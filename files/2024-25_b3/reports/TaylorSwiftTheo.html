Taylor Swift in a nutshell

Taylor Swift is an American singer-songwriter known for her narrative songwriting, which often draws from her personal life and has received widespread media coverage. Born on December 13, 1989, in West Reading, Pennsylvania, Swift began her career in country music at the age of 14. She signed with the independent label Big Machine Records and became the youngest artist ever signed by the Sony/ATV Music publishing house.

Swift has won numerous awards, including 14 Grammy Awards, an Emmy Award, and 34 American Music Awards. She is also known for her advocacy for artists’ rights within the music industry and her philanthropic efforts. Swift’s impact on popular culture and her ability to connect with fans through her music and personal storytelling have made her one of the most influential figures in contemporary music.

Taylor Swift’s Music

Having started her career at the beginning in 2003, Taylor Swift’s discography is extensive and large ranging from different genres and collaborations. Her discography is even larger because of The Scooter Braun controversy. It involved Taylor Swift’s public dispute with music manager Scooter Braun and her former record label, Big Machine Records, over the ownership of her master recordings, which Braun acquired in 2019; Swift accused Braun of bullying and manipulative behavior, leading to a highly publicized feud and her decision to re-record her earlier albums to regain control of her music.

Those 11 albums form the core musical pillars of Taylor Swift Discography. But they are far from being the only music Swift has released:

  • The Re-Recordings: Swift has already re-recorded her 6 first studio albums and released 4 starting in 2021. Each of these new albums contain the same songs as the original plus special “Vault Songs” which didn’t make the cut on the original albums. For example, Red (Taylor’s Version ) contains 10 more songs than the original, including a 10 minute version of “All Too Well.”

  • 5 Versions of the Same Album: Instead of releasing everything in one album, Swift likes to release lots of versions of the same albums. This explains why, for example, the same song “You’re On Your Own, Kid” figures 3 times in the data: in Midnights, Midnights(3am edition) and Midnights(The Til Dawn Edition)

Data Analysis - Overall Album Statistics

Let’s firstly analyze and visualize the danceability and energy of all Taylor Swift songs.

ggplot(df_final, aes(x=energy, y=danceability, color=album_name)) +

Overall, Taylor Swift songs are pretty danceable and energetic. Now the scatter plot above includes all different variations of Taylor Swift’s albums. What I wanna know is the following:

  • Is there differences between the original and re-recording of Taylor Swift albums ?

To evaluate this, I furthered filtered my data. Instead of comparing all albums, I decided to focus on comparing more specifically the original albums and their re-recordings. Midnights, Reputation, Folklore, Evermore and Taylor Swift are then not included.

# Created a filtered sub dataset for visualization
df_filtered <- df %>% filter(album_name %in% c("1989", "1989 (Taylor's Version)"))

# Plot a boxplot of the different energy levels between the original album and the Taylor Version's one
ggplot(df_filtered, aes(x=album_name, y=energy))+
  geom_boxplot()+
  labs(x="Album name", y="Energy")

The songs are the same in terms of lyrics, titles and slightly different in terms of production. But it seems like, for 1989, the new version is less energetic on average and the songs’ IQR (Interquartile range) in terms of energy is bigger than the one of the original version. Let’s check if that is true for the other albums as well.

# Change album_name from character to factor before plotting
df_filtered2 <- df %>% filter(album_name %in% c("Speak Now (Deluxe Package)", "Speak Now (Taylor's Version)"))
# Plot a boxplot
ggplot(df_filtered2, aes(x=album_name, y=energy))+
  geom_boxplot()+
  labs(x="Album name", y="Energy")

It is less true for Speak Now, with almost identical means and ranges. For Red, the means are very similar but the range is quite larger for the original version. For the re-recorded version, songs tend to be more above the average.
